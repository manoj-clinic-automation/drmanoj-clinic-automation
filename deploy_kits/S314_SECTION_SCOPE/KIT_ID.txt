WHAT IT MAKES POSSIBLE
  An orthotics-only physical stock check. Download the ordinary pad, fill ONLY the orthotic rows,
  upload it: the round is recognised as an orthotics round, is answerable for the section's items
  and no others, reports nothing not counted, and closes. Until now the same upload reported the
  other 300-odd shelves as not counted, api_pad_close refused it for staff and stamped it
  'incomplete' for the owner — and that one function, _pad_family(), feeds the upload's reply, the
  result workbook, the recent-counts list, the desk, Amir's board, the report, the hub and the loss
  board, so nothing downstream could have been right either.

HOW THE SCOPE IS DECIDED — and why it is derived rather than typed
  The pad reader has always been right about blanks: "a blank is an item NOT COUNTED", never a zero.
  So counting only the orthotic rows of the ordinary 373-row pad ALREADY records exactly those rows.
  The round therefore already says what it covers in the only way that cannot be wrong — by what was
  counted. So the scope is:
    DECLARED   stock_count.section, when the owner has set it — it wins outright;
    OBSERVED   otherwise: every item counted ACROSS THE FAMILY sits in one section of the map.
               One item outside it, or one item the map has never heard of, or a family of fewer
               than two items, and the round is a whole-shop round again.
  A PART that adds a medicine widens the whole family back to the shop, which is the truth rather
  than a convenience.

THE SAFETY VALVE
  An observed scope is an inference, and there is exactly one way it could mislead: a round MEANT to
  be the whole shop whose first sheet happened to carry only one section reads as a sectioned round
  and could be signed off 'complete' by a staff member with the rest of the shop never counted. So a
  round whose section is OBSERVED rather than DECLARED can be closed complete only by the owner. A
  declared round, and a whole-shop round, close exactly as they do today.

AND COUNT #1 KEEPS ITS SCREENS
