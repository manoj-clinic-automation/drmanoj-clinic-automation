WHAT IT DOES
  A stock line the owner marks "to pursue" becomes a CLAIM: open -> contacted -> settled.
  Darpan is asked WITH THE EVIDENCE S308 already gathered beside the line — how much of it sold
  in the thirty days before the count, or the plain fact that it did not sell once — and taps one
  of five answers in Hinglish, which carries the claim from open to contacted. The owner settles,
  and only he does: his own rule on the hub is "His answer is evidence; your tap decides."

WHY IT IS NOT JUST A NEW SCREEN
  Hub step 8 could reach only 'waiting' and 'now'. There was no door to record an answer, so once
  a line was pursued the hub's last step stayed amber for ever and the count could never visibly
  finish (S268 finding 3, from the live code). This kit is that missing door, which is why it
  closes a defect rather than adding a screen beside one.

THE SELF-CLOSE, AND WHAT IS HONEST ABOUT IT
  D471 asks the queue to close itself on a matching purchase return. IT CANNOT TODAY, and this kit
  does not pretend otherwise. Measured on the 18-Sep database: purchase_line.direction is
  'PURCHASE' on all 1,644 rows, no negative quantity, no purchase_return table; Marg prints a
  return in the item-wise report as an ordinary positive line with no marker, and the is_return
  flag marg_ingest computes lives in memory on manojz and is never persisted here. So:
    * the return rule IS WRITTEN and ASLEEP. _returns_available() is a LIVE test, not a constant,
      so the rule wakes by itself the day a return is stored — no further change here.
    * what DOES self-close today is real and stored: a LATER COUNT that finds the item agreeing.
    * a credit note (a purchase bill carrying a minus amount) is shown as a CANDIDATE and closes
      nothing. It names a supplier and an amount and NO ITEM; it can never say which line it
      answers, and it is never allowed to.
  Persisting purchase returns is F-527 and is named to the next kit.
