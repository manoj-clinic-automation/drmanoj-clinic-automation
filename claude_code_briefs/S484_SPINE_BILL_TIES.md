### 1.3 The shared bill number in a BILL-grouped sheet (anchor: the `else:` branch with `cands = by_billdate_u.get(...)` and the `if len(cands) > 1:` money test, ~l.202–206)

Keep today's test first (all lines of the number sum to exactly one candidate bill → that bill). **Add, when the PRE-filter list `by_billdate_u.get((ubill, date), [])` holds 2 or more bills and the whole-sum test did not leave exactly one** (today it leaves zero for bill 160 — the cutting must start from the pre-filter list, not from the filtered `cands`): the lines of that `(ubill, date)` group, **in sheet order** (the order of `r["data"]["lines"]`), are cut into as many contiguous non-empty runs as there are candidate bills; for every cutting and every assignment of the candidate bills to the runs, a fit is one where every run's summed `net_amount_p` is within the existing tolerance of its bill (`100 + 0.002 * abs(b["amount_p"])` paise, the same expression as the line above it). A fit is the map line → bill it produces; two fits that give every line the same bill are one fit. **If exactly one fit exists**, each line of the group ties to its run's bill. **If none or more than one fits, every line of the group stays untied** — `undated` as today, recording the pre-filter count, and the gate line *every purchase line of an export that is the authority for its whole period belongs to exactly one bill* fails as today when the sheet is the authority. Precompute the cutting once per `(ubill, date)` group before the per-line loop (a dict keyed by the line's index — `enumerate` the loop, or key by `id(ln)`) so the loop stays a lookup. A group of more than 24 lines or more than 4 candidate bills is left untied without enumeration (it then shows in the gate's own line like any untied line); the walk prints whether any group hit that cap (expected none). The SUPPLIER branch is untouched.

### 1.4 The helper (new, module-level, above `def build`)

`cut_shared_bill(lines, bills, tol)` → the dict line-index → bill, or `None` when no fit, more than one fit, or the cap — the one function §1.3 calls and §2.3 tests. Tolerance passed in as the same expression, not a new constant.
